Writing Between Deadlines

Posted on: November 21, 2026

Juggling through my time between writing, scouting for reference, and handling school deadlines (some past the deadline and just picking up scraps).

There is a strange rhythm to writing when school is constantly asking for something from you. Sometimes I have a scene in mind, a line of dialogue, or an idea that I want to follow—AAAAND now there is an assignment, a deadline, or another responsibility pulls me somewhere else. I don't always get to sit down and write when the idea is fresh. I end up returning to it in pieces, trying to remember what I wanted the moment to feel like. Now I don't discard or despite my other responsibilites, but sometimes when you are committed to something very deeply, it can disorient you... well at least for me.

Scouring for references is part of that process, too. One image can lead to another, a detail can make a scene feel more believable, and suddenly I've spent time looking for things instead of actually writing. It's useful, but man, can the sight or an inner curiosity pull you. I have to remind myself that references are there to actually make your story feel legit and not be some sort of exercise in making creative liberties that can make the audience be confused, or not replace the work of putting words on the page.

Writing between deadlines isn't ideal, and I wouldn't pretend that it always produces my best work. Still, those small scraps of time count. A paragraph written late, a note saved for later, or an idea that survives a busy week is progress—even if it doesn't look like the neat routine I wish I had.

For now, I'm learning to work with the time I have instead of waiting for the perfect time to appear. The story can keep moving, even if it moves a little at a time.

"You can't rush art!" - That one toy repair guy from Toy Story 2
