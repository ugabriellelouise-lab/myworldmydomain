Why It Is (Somehow) More Impressive To Operate Within The Box

Posted on: November 2, 2026

What I learned in committing to a single tone, seeing how it can limit you, but also be a high risk, high reward scenario.

There's something oddly satisfying about working within a limitation. It sounds counterintuitive: if you have more freedom, surely you have more room to make something interesting. But when you are committed, as in hard locked to one spot, especially it is within your comfort zone, it can look like someone being naive.

But what if someone explored all the avenues, and decided.... "Yeah... you know what? I'm gonna make something that is basically the same thing but.... better." Okay that is not the best way to explain it but.... Committing to one tone or approach means accepting that not every idea will fit. You might have to leave out a flourish, resist changing direction halfway through, or find a different way to communicate the same feeling. That can be frustrating, especially when another option seems easier or more immediately exciting. But consistency has its own reward: the pieces begin to feel connected, as if they belong to the same work.

After all, Christopher McQuarrie has demonstrated that. There is something about his Mission: Impossible works such as Rogue Nation (2015) or Fallout (2018), it may look like any other action film but it is in the subtext, the characters, how they call correlate to the scale of action, why do they correlate on the scale of action! You think Tom Cruise is out there just to be badass? No! He's always in some insane situations because half the time fate isn't kind to him when he needs to accomplish his mission!

Of course, a box can become restrictive if you treat its edges as unquestionable. The point isn't to follow a rule blindly (but of course you shouldn't go past the edge otherwise you're making something that almost barely resembles what you're aiming for); it's to understand why you chose that constraint in the first place. If it helps the work, keep it. If it stops serving the work, reconsider it. The challenge is knowing the difference between a limitation that encourages creativity and one that simply gets in the way.

That is what makes operating within the box more impressive to me. It isn't about having no choices. It's about making deliberate choices despite the limits you've decided to work with—and finding out how much you can still do inside them.
