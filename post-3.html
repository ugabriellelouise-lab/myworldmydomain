Being Someone Not What Others Seem To Expect

Posted on: July 11, 2026

Trying to live a life being a Filipino with little, fragmented experience of being in the Philippines.

Being Filipino while having only a fragmented experience of life in the Philippines is a well... quiet nowadays an understandable yet still strange thing to explain. Some parts of my identity feel familiar because of family, language, food, and the things I grew up around. Other parts feel distant because my everyday experiences have mostly been shaped elsewhere. It can make the idea of belonging feel less straightforward than people expect.

After all, my only close resemblance of something Filipino would be on food and maybe bits of my stubborn personality. But I grew up with a lot of things that you don't generally often see or appear within the space of Filipino media or culture

Sometimes there's an assumption that being Filipino means you automatically know what life back home is like, or that you can relate to every experience someone describes. I don't necessarily have that. Heck, I was 6 when I got into the Philippines boy even in my attempts to actually even talk to others, I often feel I was like sort of coping because I just feel so estranged being in here. And I was only there for a year and never came back.

People can come from the same place and still grow up with very different surroundings, routines, and perspectives. My own connection isn't absent; it's.... just I could say it comes from a different collection of experiences.

I don't think that makes me any less Filipino. It simply means my relationship with the Philippines is shaped by distance, family, the pieces I've experienced, and the things I continue to learn. I can acknowledge what I don't know without pretending that the connection isn't there.

Maybe being yourself sometimes means accepting that you won't match every expectation people have of you. You can still appreciate where you come from while being honest about the life you've actually lived. That's a balance I'm still figuring out, and I think it's worth writing about.

I am Filipino. I won't deny that at all. But I guess I could just say one little thing: I am sorry if I inconvenienced you. I mean... I guess it is fair that I pay for the bills if you suffer on blood loss from trying to speak English with me.
